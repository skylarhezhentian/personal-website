Research

Neuroscience, optimization and pure math.

  1. Jun 2026 – Present

    Neural Dynamics and Behavior Prediction

    Undergraduate Researcher, Columbia Zuckerman Institute, advised by Liam Paninski

    • Selected as 1 of 10 students for a competitive computational neuroscience research program.
    • Benchmarked lfpack and autoencoder representations of local field potentials against raw recordings, keeping neural dynamics at high temporal resolution while cutting spatial dimension by 95%.
    • Modeled simultaneous recordings across brain regions with shared and region-specific latent components, including hidden Markov models and switching linear dynamical systems.
    • Now comparing continuous, discrete and hierarchical brain-state models to test whether global states modulate regional inputs, local computations, or both, toward a dynamic brain atlas.

  2. May – Aug 2026

    Equitable Food Routing in New York City

    Student Researcher in Applied Mathematics, Laidlaw Foundation

    • Built a Python and Google OR-Tools vehicle-routing model for 528 NYC food pantries and community kitchens, with OSRM travel times, vehicle capacities and service windows, comparing five allocation policies that weigh food insecurity.
    • Need-prioritized routing raised median coverage of high-need demand from 26.3% to 57.7% with only a marginal loss of total reach, across five solves per policy and skip-penalty sensitivity tests.

  3. Jan 2026 – Present

    Directed Reading in Algebraic Topology

    Researcher, Directed Reading Program, Columbia Department of Mathematics, mentored by Azelie Picot

    • One of 10 undergraduates selected for a semester of mentored study with a PhD researcher.
    • Conducted independent, proof-based reading in algebraic topology on homotopy, fundamental groups and introductory homology, and presented the work formally at the program's closing colloquium.

  4. Jun – Aug 2024

    Abstract Algebra and Elliptic Curve Cryptography

    Student Researcher, Abstract Algebra Track, Stanford University Mathematics Camp (SUMaC)

    • One of only 64 students selected for SUMaC Program I, an intensive math camp focused on abstract algebra and number theory.
    • Collaborated with peers and graduate TAs on proof-driven problem sets, explored the homomorphism theorems and introductory Galois theory, and presented a final project applying algebraic reasoning to elliptic curve cryptography.
